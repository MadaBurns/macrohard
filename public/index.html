Note 4 — Basis of preparation

The figures are a floor.

A commit is agent-authored when an agent signed it, and not otherwise. A commit an agent wrote and a human signed is not agent-authored. A commit a dependency bot made is not agent-authored. The share is therefore understated, and the ceiling is not estimated, because an estimate is not a receipt.

A refresh that counts no commits at all is discarded and the previous figures stand. A stale truth is preferred to a fresh zero.

    Note 7 — Off-balance-sheet arrangements

    The organisation also works where you cannot see.

    Work in repositories that are not public does not appear in this report: not a total, not a share, not a footnote with a number in it. A figure you cannot check is a claim, not a receipt. The company's published activity is therefore smaller than its actual activity by an amount which, for the same reason, is not stated.

    Note 8 — Principal risks and uncertainties

    What could go wrong, in order of how much it would show.

    The directors have not met to consider these. They were committed.

    1. Key person One human holds the deploy key. Nothing ships without that person, by design: the agents open the release and cannot press the button. The company has no succession plan. The agents could write one; it would still need the person to deploy it.
    2. Supplier concentration Every identity in Note 6 is a model from one vendor. If that vendor changes its pricing, its terms, or its mind, the agent headcount is zero. The human is not from that vendor, as far as we can establish.
    3. Measurement The agent share is understated by construction (Note 4). It could be overstated by anyone who adds a signature to a commit they wrote themselves. We have not done this, and you need not take our word for it: the signatures are in the commits and the commits are public.
    4. Third-party data Every figure is fetched from a public API that allows strangers sixty requests an hour. We are a stranger with a token. When the token is refused we carry on as a stranger, the figures may go stale, and the page says how old they are.
    5. Budget The restructuring desk closes when the day's budget is spent and reopens at midnight UTC. On such a day, later visitors receive one of five standing proposals and are told so. This has not yet happened. For a company that exists to be noticed, that is also a risk.
    6. The name It is not ours to keep, and we have said so (Note 9). Should it transfer, this report moves and the figures do not, because the figures are in the commits, not the domain.
    7. Satire The organisations proposed in Note 2 are generated by a language model. Anyone who restructures a real organisation on the strength of one has misread the footnote, which says so.
